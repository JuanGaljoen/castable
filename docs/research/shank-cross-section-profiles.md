# Shank (band) cross-section profiles

**Status: SETTLED on surface definitions, THIN on numeric floors.** The trade agrees
tightly, across US and UK retail/manufacturing sources, on what each profile name
means *geometrically* — which face is flat and which is domed. It does not publish
controlled numeric figures for most of the follow-up questions (comfort-fit dome
rise, knife-edge apex minimum, graduated-shank ratio); those are recorded below as
**not found by this search**, with where the figure should have lived and what was
checked.

## 1. Per-profile surface definitions

The single clearest primary confirmation: **half-round wire is itself a stock trade
product**, and its own supplier definition is unambiguous — "one face is flat, the
other convex and rounded... the cross-section is technically a semicircle (also
called D-section)" ([Skyjems, Encyclopedia: Half-Round
Wire](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-half-round-wire); corroborated by a
second independent wire retailer's phrasing, ["one face is flat, the other convex
and rounded... combines a stable, solderable flat face with a smooth, comfortable
or decorative outer surface"](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-half-round-wire)).
This is a manufacturing-stock definition, not a marketing gloss, and it is the
anchor the rest of the table agrees with.

Two independent retail/manufacturer guides — one US-facing
([Aureus Boutique](https://www.aureusboutique.com/blogs/articles/a-guide-to-different-ring-band-styles-flat-domed-knife-edge-and-more)),
one UK trade manufacturer
([Titan Jewellery](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/)) —
and a UK retailer ([Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings))
and a jewellery-repair technical page
([Abbeville Jewellery Repairs](https://abbevilleau.com/gemstones/ring_profiles/))
converge on the same surface table:

| Profile | Inner (finger-facing) | Outer | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat / square / flat-top** | Flat | Flat, sharp edges (rectangular cross-section) | [Aureus](https://www.aureusboutique.com/blogs/articles/a-guide-to-different-ring-band-styles-flat-domed-knife-edge-and-more) ("straight, flat edges with no rounded surfaces"); [Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) ("Forms a rectangular... cross section", "edges meet at near right angles") |
| **D-shape / half-round / "domed"** | **Flat** | **Domed** (a true semicircle in the wire-stock case) | [Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) ("a cross section shaped like a capital D lying on its back" — domed outside, flat inside); [Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings) ("rounded outside and a flatter inside"); [Aureus](https://www.aureusboutique.com/blogs/articles/a-guide-to-different-ring-band-styles-flat-domed-knife-edge-and-more) calls the same shape "Domed" ("gently curved exterior and a flat interior"); wire-stock definition above |
| **Court (classic/traditional court)** | **Domed** | **Domed** | [Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) ("Both the exterior and interior are domed, creating a lens-shaped... cross section"); [Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings) ("rounded on the outside and rounded on the inside"); [Serendipity Diamonds](https://www.serendipitydiamonds.com/blog/the-many-styles-of-court-wedding-ring/) ("continuous curve from edge to edge on the outside and a matching comfort curve inside") |
| **Flat court** | **Domed** | **Flat** | [Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) ("Combines a flat exterior with a domed interior"); [Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings) ("flat outer surface and a rounded comfort-fit interior") — **the UK trade name for what US retailers call plain "comfort fit"** |
| **Shallow court** | Mostly flat, eased edges only | Domed | [Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) |
| **Knife-edge** | Softened/rounded near the ridge (not flat, not domed — see §3) | Two planes sloping up to a central ridge | [Abbeville](https://abbevilleau.com/gemstones/ring_profiles/) ("slopes away from a central ridge, forming a subtle peak"; inner "edge is typically softened for comfort"); [Aureus](https://www.aureusboutique.com/blogs/articles/a-guide-to-different-ring-band-styles-flat-domed-knife-edge-and-more) ("outer surface... rises to a sharp ridge along the middle") |
| **"Comfort fit"** | **Domed** (this is the whole definition) | **Not defined by comfort fit at all — orthogonal** | See §1a below |

### 1a. "Comfort fit" is a modifier, not an outer shape — this is the crux

**Comfort fit is defined purely by the inner surface; it says nothing about the
outer surface, and the trade explicitly pairs it with more than one outer profile.**

- Aureus states outer shape for comfort-fit bands is "**variable** (flat, domed, or
  knife-edge)" while the inner is "slightly curved on the inside, rather than flat"
  ([Aureus](https://www.aureusboutique.com/blogs/articles/a-guide-to-different-ring-band-styles-flat-domed-knife-edge-and-more)).
- Stuller's own catalogue sells both a **"14K White 6mm Half Round Comfort Fit
  Band"** and a **"Comfort-Fit Heavy Knife Edge Shoulder Shank"** as named, separate
  SKUs ([Stuller catalogue search results](https://www.stuller.com/products/details/?iid=109524)) —
  i.e. the manufacturer itself treats "half-round" / "knife-edge" (outer shape) and
  "comfort-fit" (inner shape) as independently selectable attributes, not
  alternatives.
- The UK trade's own vocabulary makes the same point under different names: **"flat
  court" is exactly a flat-outside, domed-inside band** — the UK term for what US
  retail calls a plain "comfort fit" ([Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/),
  [Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings)).
  "Court" (domed outside + domed inside) is a *different* profile from "comfort
  fit"/"flat court" even though both have a domed interior — court is domed on
  both faces, comfort-fit/flat-court is domed only on the inside.

**So, answering the brief's specific question directly: yes, comfort fit is defined
by the inner surface being domed/rounded — but no, a D-shape is not "comfort fit
under another name." A D-shape is flat inside; comfort fit (flat court) is domed
inside. They differ on exactly the surface that matters for wearing comfort, and
every source above agrees on this point without contradiction.**

## 2. Comfort fit: thickness, dome figure, sizing effect

- **Does it use more metal?** Yes, consistently stated: "since more metal is needed
  for the curved inside of a comfort fit wedding band, these bands will typically
  command a higher price than standard fit" ([Brilliant Earth, Comfort Fit vs.
  Standard](https://www.brilliantearth.com/guides/comfort-fit-ring-vs-standard/)).
  No source, however, gives this as a hard mm delta over a matched flat/D-shape band
  at the same width — it is reported qualitatively ("more metal," "heavier feel")
  across every retail source checked (Brilliant Earth, Thorum, Alpine Rings,
  RingMeasure).
- **Dome radius or rise, in mm or as a proportion of band width: not found by this
  search.** Checked: Stuller product/catalogue pages (found only product SKUs and
  widths, no dome-depth spec — the search snippet claiming "5 x 4.5mm" for one
  Stuller SKU is a shank *width x height* callout, not a dome-rise figure), Rio
  Grande wire/metals category page (returned HTTP 403, could not be fetched
  directly), and general retail/blog guidance (Brilliant Earth, Thorum, John
  Atencio, RingMeasure) — all describe the dome qualitatively ("gently curved,"
  "rounded interior") with **zero numeric radius, rise-height, or percentage given
  by any of them.**
- **Does it change effective inner diameter / sizing?** Yes, but as a *fit*
  correction, not a literal ID change: multiple sources give the same rule of
  thumb — "for most comfort fit rings under 8mm wide, subtract half a size from
  your standard measurement," while "comfort rings wider than 8mm fit like a
  standard size" ([RingMeasure, Comfort Fit Ring Size
  Guide](https://ringmeasure.com/comfort-fit-ring-size-guide/); the same half-size
  convention also appears independently at
  [Thorum](https://www.thorum.com/blogs/log/comfort-fit-men-s-wedding-bands-the-complete-guide)
  and [Alpine Rings](https://alpinerings.com/blogs/news/standard-fit-vs-comfort-fit-ring-what-s-the-difference)).
  This is presented everywhere as a **sizing convention** ("order a half size
  smaller"), not as a dimensioned ID/rise relationship a CAD tool could compute
  from a mm figure.

## 3. Knife-edge: apex minimum, sharpness in practice, angle

- **Is the apex actually sharp? No — every source that addresses this explicitly
  says it is not, and that this is intentional/expected, not a manufacturing
  defect:**
  - "there is no sharpness to the outer edge" ([Joseph Jewelry, Glossary: Knife
    Edge](https://www.josephjewelry.com/guide/glossary/knife+edge))
  - "The edge isn't actually sharp... it's just a shape that takes getting used to"
    (designer Harwell Godfrey, reported by
    [TheZoeReport](https://www.thezoereport.com/fashion/knife-edge-bands))
  - "the peak is softened enough to feel smooth against your skin" and wear over
    years further rounds the apex, which jewelers describe as desirable "character"
    (general trade guidance, multiple retailer pages,
    [TheZoeReport](https://www.thezoereport.com/fashion/knife-edge-bands),
    [Ritani](https://www.ritani.com/blogs/education/what-is-a-knife-edge-engagement-ring))
  - "A thicker band will create a slightly smoother knife edge" — i.e. sharpness
    scales inversely with overall band thickness, not a fixed apex spec
    ([JewelryShoppingGuide](https://www.jewelryshoppingguide.com/knife-edge-rings-pros-and-cons/))
- **A published minimum apex width, radius, or ridge angle for a cast knife-edge
  band: not found by this search.** Checked: retailer/glossary pages (Joseph
  Jewelry, Ritani, TheZoeReport, JewelryShoppingGuide, WillYou, WeddingKnowHow,
  Schiffman's, TeachJewelry, RobinsonsJewelers — all describe the *look and feel*,
  none gives a number), CAD-tutorial pages (CADJewelrySchool's RhinoGold knife-edge
  walkthrough — process only, zero dimensions), and general jewelry-CAD design
  guides (Honho Jewelry's design guide, which gives real numeric floors for other
  features — see below — but for knife-edge only states the qualitative rule
  **"Avoid 90° knife-edges at the top of rings unless they're thick enough and
  slightly softened"** ([Honho Jewelry, Jewelry CAD Design
  Guide](https://www.honhojewelry.com/jewelry-cad-design-guide/)) — no accompanying
  number for "thick enough").
- **Adjacent, real numeric floors from the same Honho guide** (general, not
  knife-edge-specific, offered here as the closest analogue the search surfaced):
  light ring shank minimum 1.3–1.5mm, everyday-band shank 1.6–1.8mm, wide band
  1.8–2.2mm; "no ultra-thin bridges or decorative lines under 0.6–0.7mm in metal"
  ([Honho Jewelry](https://www.honhojewelry.com/jewelry-cad-design-guide/)). These
  are shank-thickness floors in general, not an apex-specific figure — flagging the
  gap rather than reading a general floor onto knife-edge specifically.
- **Ridge angle / width:thickness proportion: not found by this search.** No source
  checked gives a numeric slope angle or a ratio of flat-face width to ridge height.

## 4. Typical width/thickness ranges per profile

Trade sources publish band **width** ranges freely but rarely tie a **thickness**
range to a *specific profile name* — thickness guidance in the sources checked is
almost always profile-agnostic ("shank thickness," not "court-profile thickness").

- General engagement-ring band width: **1.8–2.5mm** common
  ([RingMeasure](https://ringmeasure.com/comfort-fit-ring-size-guide/) summary of
  retail convention) — consistent with, and narrower than, our already-cited
  1.5–3mm / ~2.5mm typical / 2mm-minimum figures.
- **Heavy court** depth: **2.1mm** given as a specific named-weight figure for one
  UK manufacturer's "heavy court" line
  ([Titan Jewellery](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/) /
  [WeddingRings-Direct product listing, "5mm Heavy Weight Court Plain Wedding
  Ring"](https://www.weddingrings-direct.com/ring/plain-5mm-heavy-court)) — this is
  a single manufacturer's named product-line depth, not an industry-wide standard,
  and is reported as such.
- General shank-thickness floors (profile-agnostic, from Honho's CAD guide,
  repeated from §3): light 1.3–1.5mm, everyday 1.6–1.8mm, wide 1.8–2.2mm
  ([Honho](https://www.honhojewelry.com/jewelry-cad-design-guide/)).
- **No source checked publishes a thickness range broken out per profile name**
  (e.g. "D-shape bands run X–Ymm, court bands run X–Ymm") — every width/thickness
  figure found is either profile-agnostic or a single manufacturer's product-line
  spec, not a cross-profile trade table.

## 5. "Graduated" shank

**Not a distinct band-silhouette term in the sources checked — "graduated"
conventionally describes accent-*stone* sizing along a band, not the metal
cross-section or taper.** A retailer search on "graduated shank ring" surfaced only
usage like "tapered shank rings with **graduated accent diamonds**" — i.e.
graduated stones set into a tapered shank, two independent design choices bundled
in marketing copy, not one named silhouette (general retail search results, no
single authoritative page defines "graduated shank" as a shape term). By contrast
**"tapered shank" is the standard, widely used name** for a shank that narrows or
widens toward the head:
["A tapered ring shank is when the band tapers off to a thinner section, either
where the stone is set or in the opposite
direction"](https://www.cooksongold.com/blog/buying-guide/7-different-types-of-ring-shanks-and-how-to-identify-them/)
(Cooksongold, "7 Different Types of Ring Shanks").

- **Head:base width ratio, published figure: not found by this search.** Checked:
  Sightlycarat's tapered-vs-straight guide (process/measurement-protocol only, no
  ratio — explicitly states the required *measurement points*, not a target ratio:
  "record where the taper begins, its narrowest finished width and thickness...
  label finished width and thickness at the palm base, mid-shank, shoulder, taper
  start, and narrowest transition"), PriceScope forum threads ("how narrow can a
  tapered shank get" — anecdotal, forum, not treated as a spec), and Cooksongold /
  Serendipity Diamonds / Victor Canera educational pages (definitional only, no
  ratio). One anecdotal example did surface repeatedly in search summaries — "2.5mm
  at the head tapering to 1.7mm at the back" — but it could not be traced to a
  named, checkable primary page during this pass (it appeared only in an aggregated
  search-engine summary, not in the fetched page text of any source above), so it
  is **not cited as a trade figure** here; flagging its existence so a future pass
  can try to locate the underlying product page.

## 6. Which profiles suit a raised-setting engagement ring vs. a plain/wedding band

- **Knife-edge and comfort-fit solitaires are both normal, commercially available
  choices, not odd ones** — multiple retailers sell exactly this combination as a
  standard catalogue item, e.g. Blue Nile's "Knife Edge Solitaire Engagement Ring
  in Platinum" ([Blue
  Nile](https://www.bluenile.com/engagement-rings/design-your-own-ring/knife-edge-solitaire-engagement-ring-in-platinum-item-192266))
  and DiamondRensu's "Solitaire Round Cut Engagement Ring With Knife Edge Shank"
  ([DiamondRensu](https://diamondrensu.com/products/solitaire-round-cut-engagement-ring-knife-edge-shank)).
  Popularity framing across sources is consistent, though: "knife edge rings aren't
  the most common type of shank, though they have gained attention in recent years"
  (general search-summary framing across the retailer pages above) — i.e.
  **less common than plain D-shape/court, but a recognised, named, regularly
  stocked engagement-ring option, not a mismatch of profile to setting type.**
- **D-shape / half-round and court / flat-court dominate plain wedding bands** —
  these are the profiles the UK trade manufacturer and retailer pages
  ([Titan](https://titanjewellery.co.uk/info/wedding-ring-profiles/),
  [Hatton Gold](https://www.hattongold.uk/post/court-flat-court-d-shape-wedding-rings),
  [Serendipity Diamonds](https://www.serendipitydiamonds.com/blog/the-many-styles-of-court-wedding-ring/))
  frame their entire guides around, consistent with those pages being
  wedding-band-specific (not engagement-ring) buying guides.
- No source stated a hard rule that any profile is *disallowed* on a raised
  setting; the distinction found is one of relative commonality/convention, not
  prohibition.

## Confidence

- **§1 (surface definitions), including the comfort-fit-is-a-modifier finding:
  SETTLED.** Four independent sources (a manufacturing wire-stock definition, a UK
  trade manufacturer, a UK retailer, and a US style guide), plus Stuller's own
  catalogue SKU naming, converge without contradiction. This is the part of the
  brief most load-bearing for a schema, and it is the best-supported section here.
- **§2 (comfort-fit dome figure) and §3 (knife-edge apex minimum/angle): THIN
  bordering on NOT FOUND.** The qualitative claims (more metal, softened apex, not
  actually sharp) are corroborated across many independent retail sources and can
  be trusted as trade consensus. The numeric floors the brief actually needs for a
  castability gate — a dome radius/rise, an apex minimum width, a ridge angle — are
  **not published by any source checked** (retailer glossaries, UK court-profile
  manufacturer pages, CAD tutorial content, one general CAD design-floor guide).
  Trust the general profile-agnostic shank-thickness floors (Honho: 1.3–2.2mm by
  weight class) as the best available proxy for "how thick a knife-edge apex region
  needs to stay overall"; do not treat them as an apex-specific figure.
- **§4 (typical dimensions per profile): THIN.** Width ranges are well-supported and
  consistent with figures already cited in-repo. Thickness-by-profile-name is not
  published anywhere found; the one numeric depth figure (2.1mm "heavy court") is a
  single manufacturer's named product line, not an industry table.
- **§5 (graduated shank): SETTLED that it is not a distinct silhouette term** — this
  is a real, useful negative finding (the search converged on "graduated = stone
  sizing, not band shape" across a retailer search corpus), though no single
  authoritative page states the negative explicitly ("graduated is not a shank
  profile") — it is inferred from the consistent absence of any definition treating
  it as one, plus a positive, sourced definition of the term the searches keep
  substituting it for ("tapered"). The head:base ratio question is **not found**.
- **§6 (setting-type convention): SETTLED but qualitative** — the commercial
  co-occurrence of knife-edge/comfort-fit with raised solitaire settings is well
  evidenced by name-brand retailer catalogue listings; there is no numeric or rule-
  based source to cite beyond "both exist as normal stocked options, one more
  common than the other."

### Where a positive numeric answer should have lived, and what to try next

- **Comfort-fit dome rise/radius**: should live in a jewelry-CAD tutorial's
  parametric build guide (e.g. a RhinoGold/Matrix/GemVision "how to build a
  comfort-fit shank" walkthrough with a stated radius or offset), or in a
  bench-jeweler wax-carving reference. Not checked this pass: RhinoGold/Matrix/
  GemVision *product documentation* specifically (only a tutorial blog post was
  checked, and it had no numbers); the Rio Grande wire/metals page returned a 403
  and was never actually read.
- **Knife-edge apex minimum**: should live in a casting-house or 3D-print-bureau
  design-guide table (the same kind of primary source that supplied the general
  0.8mm gold wall-thickness floor already in this repo's own V-prong research) —
  specifically a Materialise or Shapeways "sharp edge / fin feature" minimum, which
  was not searched for by name this pass and is the most promising next step.
- **Graduated shank head:base ratio**: should live in a bespoke/custom jeweler's
  CAD spec sheet (the kind Sightlycarat's article gestures at with its
  "measurement points" list but does not fill in with numbers) — try a direct
  search for named CAD-approval templates from bespoke UK/US jewelers rather than
  consumer-facing retail blogs.
